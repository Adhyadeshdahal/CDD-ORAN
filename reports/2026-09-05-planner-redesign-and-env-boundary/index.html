Planner redesign, CMA-ES falsification, and the legacy/E-series boundary

Date 2026-09-05  ·  Cycle planner exploration (post-E1)  ·  Prior 2026-09-04 E1 v2 partial-correlation recovery  ·  Branches agent/planner-redesign @ 16870a8; exp/objective-ood-measure @ 067fd2f (both unmerged)
This is an archival delta report. It records what happened between 2026-09-04 (E1 recovery cleared) and 2026-09-05, on the planner / decision-value side of the project, plus a correction to an environment-numbering confusion that this cycle exposed. No frozen scientific contract was changed. All decision-utility numbers below are on the legacy v1 environments (Environment I–IV), which §6 shows are being superseded by the E1–E5 redesign.

Abstract

Directed to look for better planners, we ran a falsifiable CMA-ES gate and a three-survey method study (failure diagnosis, black-box-optimizer sweep, causal-native planner sweep). The convergent result: the planner optimizer is not the bottleneck. On the scored benchmark all default planners collapse to one decision rule over a flat, saturating, model-predicted cost; two of three legacy envs sit below the do-nothing floor; planner differences are noise. The real levers are (1) objective design, (2) out-of-distribution model trust, and (3) the world model's ability to predict joint multi-parameter interventions — a modeling capability, not a search algorithm. We built one cheap, on-thesis planner (an exact causal influence-diagram planner, CID), falsified CMA-ES covariance as buying nothing, rationalized the planner set, and — critically — established that the objective/OOD measurement work was being run against retired legacy environments, not the current E-series frontier. A ~12 h re-measurement sweep was cancelled as low-impact. Net committed artifacts are code branches held unmerged pending review; this cycle's value is the diagnosis and the boundary correction.

1. What this cycle set out to do

Following the 2026-09-04 E1 recovery result, the project owner redirected effort to the planner side: (a) reclassify QACM from an ensemble member to a third-party baseline; (b) explore CMA-ES / MPC and keep only planners that fit the causal learned graph and earn their cost in a resource-constrained RIC. The animating concern: the existing planners performed poorly across the legacy environments, and weeks of effort had yielded mostly infrastructure and negative results. This report is the archival record of that exploration and its (clarifying) outcome.

2. The CMA-ES gate — a falsifiable test

We isolated the optimizer under a planning oracle (true next-KPI model, so world-model error is removed as a confound), matched budget (256 candidates × 10 iters), and scored each joint action by realised true utility on the EnvI/train coordination needle (kpi2 = exp(−(P1−50)²/(2·P3)²), non-separable — needs P1 and P3 moved together), 6 seeds × 8 steps = 48 decisions.

Table 1 — CMA-ES gate. Utility z-score (do-nothing floor = −0.1257; coarse-grid 41³ = +4.3237).
ArmWhat it addsUtilityms / decision
JointMultiNCP (shipped)sequential per-NCP, K calls+0.114485.9
DiagCMAES (= joint-CEM)joint-param eval, diagonal+4.938052.5
CausalBlockCMAESjoint-param eval + block covariance+4.909537.0

Attributing each design choice separately:

covariance: BlockCMAES − DiagCMAES = −0.0285 (±0.0427) → zero within spread eval scheme: DiagCMAES − JointMultiNCP = +4.8236 → all the gain lives here
Verdict: CMA-ES covariance earns its place = NO. The block covariance — the thing that makes it CMA-ES rather than plain CEM — buys nothing (−0.03). The entire win is joint-parameter evaluation (evaluating the block's knobs together, one model call per candidate), which a plain diagonal joint-CEM already has. The ~40–57% latency cut vs the shipped Joint is real, but it is a joint-eval effect (batched calls), not a covariance effect.

The catch that reframes the finding: the +4.82 is under an oracle. The shipped JointMultiNCP parks near the floor (+0.11) because it applies the block's NCPs sequentially, feeding only predicted KPIs forward and never forming the combined parameter vector (joint.py:131-158) — a deliberate out-of-distribution avoidance, because the world model (CDL) was trained on single-parameter interventions. So joint multi-param evaluation is OOD on the trained model. The coordination lever is a world-model capability, not the optimizer.

3. Three-survey synthesis — the planner is not the bottleneck

Three parallel surveys — a failure diagnosis grounded in reports/, a black-box-optimizer sweep, and a causal-native planner sweep — converged. On the scored harness every default planner (QACM · CEM · MPPI · MCTS) collapses to one decision rule: single-parameter, single-step, grid-argmin over a flat, saturating, model-predicted cost. Conflicts are scored independently and proposals are never chained. The consequences and their causes:

Bottom line. Swapping the search cannot move the number — on a flat landscape every optimizer ties. The levers are objective design, OOD trust, and world-model multi-intervention capability. This is the third independent finding pointing at model/discovery quality rather than the planner.

Keep / Explore / Cut (merged across both method surveys)

  • Build Causal influence-diagram planner (CID) — exact enumeration of the conflict param's lattice, single-param in-distribution scoring, argmax expected-utility (§4).
  • Keep Integer-margin on the diagonal CEM/CMA (stops variance collapse on the integer lattice); QACM → market baseline; scored do-nothing floor.
  • Explore Causal-block-gated joint sampling and single-node causal-BO (fallback; GP budget risk) — both gated on the world-model multi-intervention question.
  • Cut full/dense CMA-ES covariance; MCTS·RHCEM → ablation flags; LM-MA-ES · IPOP/BIPOP · GP-BO · TuRBO · SMAC · diffusion/gradient-MPC (wrong regime / heavy deps / OOD-gated).

4. CID — the one planner worth building

CausalInfluenceDiagramPlanner (cdd_oran/planners/cid.py, branch agent/planner-redesign @ 16870a8, unmerged). For the given conflict parameter it exactly enumerates that parameter's bin×index lattice (50–210 leaves), scores every leaf with one batched single-parameter, in-distribution structure-conditioned world-model call, and takes argmax expected utility with a deterministic tie-break. It is the exact form CEM/MPPI approximate, but sampler-free, GP-free, and it never issues a joint multi-param query — so it dodges the OOD trap.

  • Exactness verified against an independent brute-force grid argmin (PASS); single-param-only and CRN-reproducible tie-break (PASS).
  • Integer-margin added to the diagonal CMA/CEM fallback (cma_es.py): floors each dim's effective std to ≥ one lattice step (PASS).
  • Latency single-digit to low-tens of ms/decision at the real lattice — within the RIC budget. Full suite 392 passed, 1 xfailed.
Honest limit. On the current single-parameter scored path CID's argmin agrees closely with the QACM baseline — it is the exact version of what QACM approximates. CID only differentiates once (a) the objective fix makes argmax-EU actually climb (FM-1) and (b) a graph-pruning layer lets it choose which / how few parameters to move. Necessary, not sufficient. An adversarial review of this branch was in progress at report time.

5. Objective / OOD measurement — and why the sweep was cancelled

We validated the measurement pipeline by reproducing the pinned legacy Env I baseline exactly (QACM = −0.08319 vs pinned −0.083). The two fixes with the largest proven ROI are already implemented but off by default: the utility_weight gradient (validated λ=0.25) and the ensemble-disagreement penalty (validated value 1 — note the brief's ood_threshold is a flag only and decision-inert). 6. The correction: legacy Environment I–IV vs the E1–E5 redesign

This cycle exposed a numbering confusion worth recording permanently. There are two environment systems, and the diagnosis (which read the older reports/) followed the legacy one:

E2's state, precisely: the environment is built and committed; the nonlinear label-free discovery method is written, tested, and banked (e2slice/) — but never run. At the frozen constants the run is memory-bandwidth-bound (~5–18 days on the current ~16 GiB box), so it is a standing TODO for a larger machine. Until it runs we cannot answer E2's discovery question. The E2 decision gate is a preregistered recorded null (frozen), and decision-value results stay uninterpretable until discovery clears its gate. "Completing E2" = running that one parked discovery job on adequate hardware — a compute constraint, not missing code.

7. Legacy / E-series code boundary (dependency map)

The legacy envs are logically isolable — no E-series code path calls the legacy get_env or the ORANEnvironment* classes; the v2 envs, e1/e2slice, and the V2-native objective (analysis/v2_regret.py) and sequence planner (planners/sequence.py) form a self-contained path. But there are three import-time seams that a naive deletion would break:

  1. planners/cost.py:6 module-imports envs.base.XApp (used by all legacy planners; not by the E-series). Note envs/base.py primitives are distinct from env_i..iv.
  2. analysis/threshold_sweep.py:19 imports get_env; recovery_metrics.py:22 imports threshold_sweep; the E-series evaluators import recovery_metrics — so importing E1/E2 evaluate transitively loads envs/__init__.py, which eagerly imports env_i..iv. This is the confusion-breeding seam.
  3. config.py:217 loads configs/env_i_mlp.yaml at import; config.py:106 types environment to the legacy Literal only.

8. Decisions and recommendations

  • CMA-ES covariance: cut. Kept only as a documented, reproducible negative result + the gate harness.
  • Planner set: rationalized (QACM → baseline, scored do-nothing, MCTS/RHCEM → ablation flags, keep MPPI+CEM+Joint+CID). Held unmerged pending review.
  • CID: keep as a live thread; its real upgrade is the graph-pruning (which/how-few params) layer + the world-model multi-intervention question — not further optimizer tuning.
  • The real planner lever is the objective (FM-1) and the world model's multi-intervention capability (FM-3). These should shape the E-series decision layer; they are not a reason to re-measure legacy envs.
  • Legacy quarantine (recommended, not yet done): do not hard-delete now. Instead: (i) add deprecation markers clarifying legacy vs E-series; (ii) break seam #2 so the E-series stops transitively loading env_i..iv (move the pure _prf metric off threshold_sweep, or make envs/__init__ lazy) — keeping all 440 tests green; (iii) put the status markers where they belong — E3/E4 as scaffolded (no slice yet) and E5 as research-only — not "not implemented" on the legacy envs (which are implemented but retired). Defer the full delete (it touches cost.py, config.py, and ~8 tests) until the E-series fully supersedes.
Next. Complete E2 (run the parked discovery job when hardware allows); carry the objective + world-model-multi-intervention levers into the E-series decision layer; land the legacy quarantine hygiene; revisit CID's graph-pruning layer once E2 recovery is known.
