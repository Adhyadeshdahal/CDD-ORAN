# Strategy after the E6-scn-v1 Gate A kill

**Do not spend 20–25 CPU-hours on the full v2-xApp Gate A next.** On unchanged whole-episode SVR, a pass is improbable beyond v1's defects. Across four exploratory surge tapes, only 0.5–1.6% of violated eMBB UE-seconds were relievable within 4 dB CIO (3.5% is the *base-medium* maximum); erasing every in-disk event violation still misses 15% whole-episode relief on three tapes. Perfect mistune repair removes an estimated 1.4–7.4% of violated UE-seconds. Better adjacency and MRO correction may improve *own KPIs*, not generate 35% removable SVR. This small-sample move-the-violator diagnostic omits gains to UEs left behind and is no impossibility theorem. Yet a 2,800-episode rerun has low decision value.

**Is running it necessary?** Only to claim *confirmatorily* that v2 fails (or passes) the original gate. Prediction from inspected DEV diagnostics is not a test. If that claim matters, freeze v2, use untouched registered seeds, run every arm without filtering, and publish beside v1. Otherwise stop the v1 strong-SLA route now. Mechanics-only checks and an exploratory fresh-seed ceiling/first-stage study can decide whether to fund confirmation; neither can declare a pass or change the rule. The capacity classification is not a bound on all interventions.

**Scenario change can be legitimate.** A border hotspot beside spare capacity, heterogeneous layer or coupled ES/SLICE/MLB demands can represent real conflicts. It is tailoring if geometry, intensity, duration or denominator are chosen until this arbiter wins, or headroom requires inaccessible centralised writes. Ground physical mechanism and prevalence externally; cover varied geometries, loads and failures, not selected “relievable” seeds. Freeze generation, WG3 rights/budgets, whole-episode and event-local metrics, baselines, guardrails and seeds *before* candidate outcomes. Event-local success is narrower; retain whole-episode SVR and v1 controls. Establish same-rights budgeted oracle headroom before training; oracle is privileged.

## Ranked paths for the requested strong edge

1. **New externally grounded, conflict-rich benchmark + genuinely better deployable WG3 controller: highest ceiling, highest evidence burden.** Demonstrate that ordinary strong xApps each meet their own KPI, their simultaneous operation causes avoidable SLA loss, and a *budgeted* WG3 oracle has ample margin over the best goal-keeping baseline across loads. Then a learned policy-effect controller must close enough of that margin on untouched tapes to beat faithful QACM, Sharma, Djidjev–Kaminski, two-tower, PACIFISTA and GNN implementations under identical observations, training data, intervention rights, latency and churn; retain guardrails, paired uncertainty and out-of-distribution checks. Verify those comparators' actual applicability and reproduce rather than assume their performance. If their rights differ, report both native-task and equal-rights comparisons. This is a new benchmark and claim, not v1 redemption.
2. **Other existing environment with demonstrated conflict-controllable headroom: potentially faster route.** First screen for unavoidable floor, xApp own-KPI competence and a same-rights oracle gap, then freeze an independent benchmark and compare the full published set on closed-loop, held-out episodes. E2-CL or any existing setting only qualifies if the gain is broad, repeatable and robust to realistic budgets—not a single favourable constraint or privileged rollout. This may beat designing E6 physics to order.
3. **Repair v2 xApps on unchanged E6-scn-v1 and rerun Gate A: scientifically clean, low probability of the requested edge.** Fix radio adjacency and xApp mechanisms, freeze and test if you want the negative/positive control documented. Even an own-KPI win does not meet the original whole-episode 0.85/0.35 gate; Gate B, learned-vs-oracle gap and published-comparator superiority would remain. Do not let a surprisingly good Gate B oracle retroactively waive Gate A.
4. **Headroom-by-action-class + constrained/Pareto paper: most defensible near-term pivot, but not the user's specified strong SLA edge.** Quantify capacity floor, xApp harm, bounded WG3 versus direct-SON/oracle ceilings, collection/intervention cost and guardrail tradeoffs across E6 and E2-CL; separate privileged ceilings from deployable controller wins. A compelling methodological or diagnostic contribution is possible, but call it that. If only this survives, say the strong-edge objective is unmet rather than relabel parity as victory.
