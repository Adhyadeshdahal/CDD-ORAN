# E6 decision: stop trying to rescue the current scenario with a slogan

**My choice: option 2, conditional on a short, predeclared feasibility test; keep E6 as built as a negative/control scenario.** Do not start training a causal world model yet. The observed opportunity is mostly to undo xApp damage: freeze is already better than noarb on SVR, TS barely helps SLA, and MRO is nearly inert. An arbiter cannot manufacture a large *positive* SLA benefit from those requests. Design one independently motivated hotspot/surge regime in which steering and mobility really can prevent violations, while energy saving and slice allocation can interfere. Preserve the original regime and report both. If that regime cannot create substantial, *feasible* coordination headroom, change the contribution or task rather than shopping for a convenient simulator.

The oracle results are evidence against **this investment**, not a mathematical ceiling: candidate search is restricted, receding-horizon decisions are myopic, and DEV has only 3–5 seeds per load. But even privileged per-region search gets only 0.924× freeze at medium and 0.976× at high load on the pure-SLA objective. The priced version is 0.960/0.984× freeze. Learning will consume, not add, that margin. More importantly, the modify/write diagnostics used `write_budget=1e9` (`scratchpad/e6_dev/grid.py`), whereas the environment defaults to 300 writes/scored hour, **50 per 600-second run**. Round 2 reports 296–545 writes. Its apparent Pareto advantage is therefore not evidence that a deployable controller can achieve it. The previous E2-CL result that passive response models rank actions worse than accept-all compounds the problem.

## Hostile-reviewer objections

- **1 — Pareto reframe on current E6:** “You changed the promised endpoint after seeing that your SLA advantage disappeared against an embarrassingly simple freeze policy. Energy and LL improvements are interesting, but your privileged, over-budget oracle is not your method; where are the deployable-policy results and genuinely competitive multi-objective baselines?” This can become an honest *different* paper if those results exist; it does not meet the requested strong-SLA claim.
- **2 — Redesign scenario:** “You tuned synthetic hotspots, traffic and xApps until your own planner won. Are the surge distribution, intervention costs, alternative controllers, and test regimes externally justified? Does the result survive the original regime and shifted hotspot timing/location?” This is the best bet only with a fixed scenario rationale and untouched holdouts.
- **3 — Search harder for an oracle:** “You spent compute increasing oracle privilege and action search, then called its return a ceiling. Can your observed-information, write-budgeted learner exploit any of it?” One bounded feasibility probe is useful; open-ended CEM/JAX optimization is a distraction until the task has positive coordination value.
- **4 — Change metric/task/contribution:** “Did you move the goalposts after looking at DEV? What exactly does the new claim establish about preventing SLA violations?” Legitimate if stated plainly as a new hypothesis, not quietly sold as the old one.

## The bar

Keep **≤0.75× every comparator, including freeze**, as the aspirational meaning of the user's *unqualified* strong-SLA edge. Freeze is a necessary sanity comparator even though published QACM-like papers may omit it. But distinguish that claim from **strong SLA among goal-retaining policies**, for which freeze is ineligible and must still be shown as a separate SLA/energy/goal reference. Do not claim “beats every solution” from beating wrappers that may not faithfully reproduce the published methods. `E6_METRIC.md` currently mixes a 0.75 point-estimate bar with a one-sided test against 0.80; that is a defensible descriptive margin plus evidential threshold, but it is **not** statistical evidence that the true ratio is ≤0.75. State the distinction. Freeze failing goal retention cannot make its SVR vanish from the plot. Goal retention also needs a stable absolute-margin rule when the noarb–freeze KPI difference is near zero; otherwise a 90% fraction is noise. Precommit comparisons and scenario selection before TEST.

## One-week go/no-go

1. **Days 1–2:** Specify one surge mechanism from an operational workload rationale (local arrival/traffic burst, duration, spatial footprint, and onset uncertainty) *before* examining its outcomes. Freeze parameters and seed split. Keep the original E6 cells as a control; report solo TS/MRO, noarb, freeze, and a tuned static subset. Enforce the actual write budget and actuator limits for all policy runs.
2. **Days 3–5:** Run paired medium/high-load DEV episodes with varied surge timing/location. First test whether TS/MRO solo produce material SLA gains against freeze and whether their combination with ES/SLICE creates a recoverable conflict. Then run a privileged, budget-constrained joint-action lookahead as a **feasibility witness**, not an upper bound. Measure SVR, per-slice violations, RLF, severe incidents, energy and goal retention on the *same tapes*. Prefer the specified 30-minute scored episode; if using 10 minutes for triage, label it provisional and scale the budget correctly.
3. **Days 6–7:** Freeze the scenario and decide. **Kill this redesign as the route to a 25%-vs-freeze claim** if the budgeted privileged witness cannot reach ≤0.75× freeze in **both** load strata on the prespecified DEV split while passing the guardrails and retaining the positive xApp goals, or if the result requires selecting a favourable surge parameter/seed after inspection. This failure is a no-go for investment, **not proof of an impossibility bound**. If it passes, test a real observed-information policy and stronger baselines on untouched seeds before claiming any edge. If it fails, pursue an explicitly multi-objective/constraint-satisfaction contribution or a different externally grounded end task; do not dilute “strong SLA edge” into Pareto wording.
